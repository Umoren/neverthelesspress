## Notes

This issue has two strong doors in.

The first door is loneliness. That is probably the more immediate one because almost everyone understands the feeling of being surrounded and still alone. It gives the piece a concrete human problem before it starts tracing theology.

The second door is the shock claim itself: many believers know Jesus as Lord, King, Redeemer, Savior. Fewer really live with the thought that he also calls them friend. That claim is strong, but it has to be handled carefully so the writing does not become sentimental or overfamiliar.

The sermon already gives a clean structure through three timelines:

1. The Garden
2. The fathers and prophets
3. The incarnation and the explicit words of Jesus

That structure is useful because it shows that friendship with God is not a random New Testament mood. It is part of a long divine pattern.

The closing movement should not stay abstract. The sermon ends with imitation, and the issue probably should too. If Jesus is friend in this way, then Christian friendship should look different from ordinary networking, clout-chasing, or selective affection.

The voice reference sharpens the path further. Samuel is strongest when the writing begins with an actual situation, names the tension plainly, and only then widens into theology. For this issue, that probably means opening with loneliness, social noise, or the feeling of being surrounded and still alone before making the doctrinal claim.

The issue should also avoid sentimental language. "Jesus is your friend" can easily become soft church talk if the writing does not hold the paradox firmly. The stronger line is that Jesus truly befriends people without ceasing to be Lord. That distinction needs to stay alive all through the piece.

## Proposed issue structure

This should be a mixed pastoral and theological piece. The pastoral side gets the reader in. The theological side keeps the issue from turning into soft church language.

### Working title options

1. The Friend Jesus
2. You Did Not Just Receive a Savior
3. Not Servants Only
4. The God Who Came Near

The cleanest title is still `The Friend Jesus`. It is simple, specific, and direct.

### Hero

Purpose: open with the ache before the answer.

Direction:
- Begin with loneliness, not doctrine.
- Keep the sentence concrete.
- Do not open with "friendship with God" as a concept.

Possible angle:
- You can have attention and still not have friendship.
- You can be known by many people and still feel profoundly alone.

The hero claim should land by the end of this opening movement:

`When you received the gospel, you did not just receive a Lord and Savior. You received a great friend.`

### Spread 1

Section idea: `The loneliness problem`

Purpose: make the human problem visible before theology starts doing work.

Direction:
- Talk about the difference between social noise and actual friendship.
- Use the sermon's observation that people can be surrounded and still be lonely.
- Keep it adult. No melodrama.

This section should raise the real need: not more people around you, but someone who truly knows you and still comes near.

### Spread 2

Section idea: `God was never interested in distance`

Purpose: establish the first timeline.

Scriptures:
- Genesis 2:15-22
- Genesis 3:8-9

Direction:
- Show that nearness was there from the beginning.
- God walking in the garden matters.
- God calling first after Adam and Eve hid matters.

Main line:

`The God of the gospel did not begin with distance and then slowly become warm. Nearness was there from the start.`

### Spread 3

Section idea: `Before Jesus said it, God had been building toward it`

Purpose: establish the fathers and prophets timeline.

Scriptures:
- Hebrews 1:1-2
- Exodus 33:11
- James 2:23
- Isaiah 41:8
- Jeremiah 9:24

Direction:
- God spoke.
- Moses was spoken to as one speaks to a friend.
- Abraham was called God's friend.

This section should make one point clearly: friendship with God is not a random New Testament flourish. It has a history.

### Spread 4

Section idea: `Then the Word moved into the neighborhood`

Purpose: bring the argument into the incarnation.

Scriptures:
- John 1:14
- John 15:12-15

Direction:
- This is where the issue should tighten.
- Jesus does not only reveal God. He embodies the nearness God had been moving toward all along.
- He explicitly says, "I no longer call you servants... I have called you friends."

This is probably the doctrinal center of the issue.

### Spread 5

Section idea: `Friend, but not your equal`

Purpose: protect the issue from sentimentality.

Direction:
- Make the distinction clearly.
- Jesus is friend, but not buddy.
- The friendship is real. The lordship is also real.

This section needs careful wording because it is where the issue either becomes mature or becomes soft.

Main line:

`The friendship is intimate, but it is not casual. He draws near without becoming small.`

### Spread 6

Section idea: `The proof of friendship is the cross`

Purpose: define what makes Jesus the greatest friend.

Scriptures:
- John 15:13
- 1 John 3:16

Direction:
- The issue should not leave friendship vague.
- The standard is not companionship alone. It is sacrificial love.
- No human friendship reaches that height.

Main line:

`No friendship is greater than one that bleeds for you.`

### Spread 7

Section idea: `What his friendship should produce in us`

Purpose: move from receiving friendship to imitating it.

Scriptures:
- Romans 12:15
- John 13:35

Direction:
- Genuine care without transaction.
- Love without class calculation.
- Truth without performance.
- Presence in people's joy and pain.

This is where the issue becomes practical without becoming list-heavy too early.

### Spread 8

Section idea: `How do you become a friend of God?`

Purpose: close the theological loop.

Scriptures:
- James 2:23

Direction:
- Abraham became God's friend by faith.
- Do not make this sound merit-based.
- The point is not moral impressiveness. The point is trust.

This section should keep the gospel clear.

### Closer

Purpose: end with one reframing line, not a sermon recap.

Direction:
- Return to loneliness.
- Return to the claim.
- Let the issue end with nearness, not with summary.

Possible closing direction:
- Some people are not starved of attention. They are starved of true friendship.
- The gospel answer is not that Jesus tolerates you from a throne. It is that he calls you near.
- Not servant only. Not subject only. Friend.

## Raw Fragments

- You can have attention and still not have friendship.
- Some people know Jesus as authority and rescuer, but not as one who draws near.
- Friendship with Jesus is warm, but it is not casual.
- The God who walked in the garden is the God who came near in Christ.
- Not servant only. Not subject only. Friend.
